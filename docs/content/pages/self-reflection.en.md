# Self-Reflection That Ends in Evidence

Self reflection is the most recommended thing you can do and the easiest to do without ever
finishing. People write about their feelings for a week, feel clearer for two days, and then have
nothing in hand three weeks later. That is not a discipline problem. It is a structure problem:
most of what is sold as reflective journaling produces a mood and no claim.

The version worth keeping produces one artefact: a sentence you can check later.

## Three signs the reflection is spinning

1. **Emotion, no event.** Pages about how it felt, and not one dated thing that happened. Feelings
   are legitimate data but they cannot be cross-checked against anything, so nothing accumulates.
2. **Today, no span.** A reflection that only covers the last 48 hours cannot be contradicted by
   evidence, because it has not given evidence time to arrive.
3. **Conclusion, no next step.** "I need to set better boundaries" is not a conclusion; it is a
   wish wearing the grammar of one. A conclusion names something observable that will differ next
   week — or admits it will not.

If all three are present, the session was a relief valve, which is fine, but it is not this.

## The loop, in the order it actually runs

Reflection is the middle of a four-step loop, not a standalone exercise. Skipping the first step
is why most **self-reflection questions** produce nothing: they ask you to interpret a blank.

| Step | What it asks | What it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|
| Record | What happened | A dated sentence in your own words |
| Check-in | How the day sat | A number you can plot, not a story |
| Reflect | What I read into it | A claim: "I think X, because of Y" |
| Return | What actually happened | A correction you did not have to invent |

The last row is the one almost no journal offers. Without it, the reflection never becomes
anything other than your own opinion of yourself, restated at intervals — and the restatement is
why people say reflection does not work for them.

## The weekly template

**The weekly five-liner: what happened, what I read into it, what I did, what came of it, one change next week.**

Five lines, weekly, in this order. Not a journal — a form. Do it in eight minutes or not at all.

| Line | Constraint | Weak version | Usable version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. What happened | Only what a camera could catch | "It was a heavy week." | "Monday review ran 90 minutes; I said nothing in it." |
| 2. What I read into it | Mark it as your reading | "I'm invisible at work." | "I read that as them not wanting my input." |
| 3. What I did | A verb, or write "nothing" | "Tried to speak up more." | "Sent one follow-up note on Tuesday." |
| 4. What came of it | Blank is allowed | — | "No reply. Agendas now circulate a day early." |
| 5. One change next week | One, observable | "Be more assertive." | "Open with a sentence before the third item." |

Line 2 is where the discipline lives: it separates the event from your interpretation, which is
the only move that makes line 4 falsifiable later. Line 4 being blank is a legitimate answer, and
recording that blank is worth more than a confident reading of it.

## Why prompts and templates are different objects

A prompt solves "I don't know what to ask". A template solves "I asked and nothing came of it".
If you are short on questions, [the 30-prompt library](/en/journal-prompts/) is sorted by the job
each prompt does — that page is the material, this page is the structure you put it into.

If you already write and it dissipates, the missing piece is not more questions; it is the return
date. [Self improvement you can audit](/en/self-improvement/) covers the Goal → Action → Outcome
version of the same loop.

## A boundary worth stating twice

Reflection is not a substitute for talking to a clinician, and this page is not medical advice:
a five-line form cannot assess you, and no pattern in your own writing is an assessment of
yourself.
Rumination and reflection look identical from the inside and differ in one observable thing —
whether the session converges on a next step. If yours is not converging, if sleep is involved,
or if entries are turning toward hurting yourself, that is a reason to involve a person:
a crisis line or emergency service in your country, or someone you trust. Our screens put that
path before any suggestion to keep writing.
