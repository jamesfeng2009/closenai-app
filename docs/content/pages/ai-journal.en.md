# What an AI Journal Should Show You

An **ai journal** is a record system that can point back at your own words. Not a chatbot that
replies to them. The difference is not a feature list — it is what the product keeps after you
close the app, and whether a later sentence about you can be traced to an earlier sentence
you wrote.

That test is the reason this page exists. If you are deciding whether to keep a year of your
writing in an AI journaling tool, the useful question is not "is it smart" but "when it says
something about me, can I see the receipts".

## First, the definition we actually build to

A journal entry is yours. A mood check-in is yours. What an AI layer adds is the ability to
notice that Tuesday's entry and the next Friday's entry are about the same thing — and to show
both lines when it says so.

If the tool cannot show both lines, it is not an insight. It is a mood of a paragraph.

So our definition of AI journal quality is narrow and checkable:

1. Every stored statement about you is either **your text** or **a claim that links to your text**.
2. Silence is not evidence. If you did not write it, the system does not infer it.
3. The link is inspectable: open it, read the original sentence, and see the date it was written.

## The loop the page is really about

The reason the phrase "with AI" matters is that a diary alone gives you one column of data and
most people stop there. The loop we build is four steps, and each step has a different owner.

| Step | What gets written | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| Record | The entry itself — what happened, in your words | You |
| Check-in | One tap for how the day sat, plus optional words | You |
| Reflection | A question answered, and what you concluded from it | You, prompted |
| Outcome | What actually happened to that conclusion, later | You, when the date arrives |

An AI layer that only produces the third row is a writing aid. One that produces the fourth row
is a record you can audit — and that is the part that changes behaviour, because the
third row is a guess and the fourth row is a correction.

## "It keeps what you wrote" is not the same claim as "it keeps you"

Marketing for this category leans on a sense of being held in mind. We do not use that framing,
and we do not think you should accept it from anyone. Two claims look similar and are not:

- **Storage claim:** the app indexes entries you authored, so it can retrieve them later. This is
  measurable. You can ask it to find something and see whether it does.
- **Mind claim:** the app knows you, in the way a person would. This is not measurable, and it
  is where expectations get set up to be disappointed.

We build to the first claim only. Practically: memory rows carry an `origin` — you stated it, you
did something, or a structured event produced it. A quiet week does not become a conclusion about
you. If you want to journal with AI and keep that boundary, ask the vendor one question: *what
does your system write down when I write nothing?*

## The template on this page

**What the AI did / what the AI did not do — a two-column table written from our own data model.**

Use it on any product in this category, including ours, before you commit a year of entries to it.

| What it did | What it did not do |
|---|---|
| Pulled evidence lines out of your entries and attached them to a claim | Decide whether your week went well |
| Ordered what you already wrote so a pattern is comparable across weeks | Fill a gap in your record with an assumption |
| Asked a follow-up question when a conclusion had no evidence attached | Speak as a person, or pretend to be one |
| Surfaced an old entry when a new one echoed it | Send your text out to be modelled somewhere else |
| Kept a "result unknown" state when you had not reported back | Replace the unknown with a number |

If a product cannot fill the right-hand column honestly — if every row is a capability — it is
selling the mind claim while delivering the storage claim.

## How to check one insight, concretely

Open the insight. Expand it. You should see: the original sentence, the date it was written, and
which entry it came from. Read the sentence. If it is not what the insight claims, you can
overturn it — the claim is a row in a table, not a verdict.

If an insight has no expandable evidence, our own data model keeps it as nothing: the pattern
field stays empty rather than holding a plausible-sounding sentence.

## Boundaries

This describes a record-keeping tool. It is not medical advice, not a clinical assessment, and
nothing on this page should be read as one. If what you are writing about includes thinking about
hurting yourself, the app is the wrong place to put that — stop reading this page and contact a
crisis line or emergency service in your country, or someone you trust. On our screens, that
kind of entry gets the help path placed before any product suggestion.

We do not claim to remember what you did not write, and we do not describe this as companionship.
