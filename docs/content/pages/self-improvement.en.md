# Self-Improvement You Can Audit

> Other journals ask how you feel. ClosenAI asks how it actually went.

That sentence is the whole difference, and it is testable, because "how it actually went" is a
question with an answer you do not yet have on the day you write it down.

Most **self improvement** advice is written from the perspective of someone who has just decided.
That is the moment with the most energy and the least information. Six weeks later the energy is
gone and the information has arrived, and almost no tool is built to hand it back to you. A habit
tracker tells you that you showed up on Tuesday. It cannot tell you that the thing you showed up
for did not work.

## Goal → Action → Outcome, in one line each

| Term | One-line definition | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | The thing you want to be different, with a date attached | "Someday" plans that can never be wrong |
| Action | The one concrete thing you do this week | Effort that feels like progress |
| Outcome | What actually happened, reported later by you | A plan judged by intent instead of result |

The reason a lot of personal growth tracking fails is that it stores two of the three. It keeps the
goal (aspirational) and the action (streaks, checkboxes), and the outcome column is either missing
or filled in by the app on your behalf — which is where the lying starts.

Our outcome rows default to `unknown`. Not as humility theatre: there is no field that could hold
a score, and the schema only accepts an outcome you stated, an action you took, or a structured
event. A silent fortnight stays silent in the record, which is a truer sentence about your fortnight
than any inference we could produce.

## The expectation-vs-actual box

**The expectation-vs-actual box: what I expected, what actually happened, the gap, one change.**

Write it before the week starts, fill it after. This is the template the whole page exists to hand
you — four boxes, about six minutes.

| Box | Rule | Weak entry | Usable entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. I expected | Must be falsifiable, with a number or an event | "I'll be more consistent." | "I'll finish two writing blocks before 9pm on Mon and Wed." |
| 2. What happened | Facts only, no self-commentary | "Kind of missed it." | "Monday yes. Wednesday started at 22:40, stopped after 8 minutes." |
| 3. The gap | Name where the plan was wrong | "I lack discipline." | "The plan assumed 9pm; the meeting ran late both weeks." |
| 4. One change | Something to test next week, not a resolution | "Try harder." | "Block at 7am instead, and see if Wednesday holds." |

Box 3 is where learning happens and where almost everyone puts a character judgement instead of a
mechanical one. "I lack discipline" cannot be tested next week. "The plan assumed 9pm" can.

## What a self improvement plan looks like in practice

One goal per week. Not five. The format only produces information if you come back to it, and
people come back to one box, not to a dashboard.

Then the follow-up happens: the date arrives, the app asks the same question you asked yourself in
box 1, and you answer in box 2. That round trip is the feature. Everything else in a
self improvement plan — the streaks, the badges, the quarterly review decks — is machinery for
making you feel like you are doing the thing.

## Boundaries

This is a description of a record-keeping tool, not a method that improves anything by itself.
Measurement does not change a number; the change comes from what you do after you see it. Nothing
here is not medical advice, and no box on this page is an assessment of you — we do not claim to know
whether your week was good, only to keep what you said about it.

If the reason you are looking for self improvement is that you are not safe with your own
thoughts — including thoughts of hurting yourself — a form is the wrong next step. Contact a
crisis line or an emergency service in your country, or a person you trust. Our screens place that
path before any product suggestion.
